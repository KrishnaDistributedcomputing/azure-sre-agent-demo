Must-have control requirements

Open each control to review mandatory evidence
Customer engagement record
  • Customer organization, approved stakeholders, and decision authority
  • Business objective, expected outcome, scope, and exclusions
  • Tenant, subscription, region, environment, and data boundary
  • Assumptions, constraints, dependencies, risks, milestones, and owners
  • Communication, escalation, success measures, and acceptance authority
  • Every material interaction recorded within two business days
Meeting and interaction evidence
  • Date, attendees, organizations, roles, and authority
  • Purpose, customer context, discussion, and requested outcomes
  • Decisions linked to decision records
  • Actions with owner, due date, status, and closure evidence
  • Risks, blockers, mitigation, escalation, and customer confirmation
Technical decision records
  • Context, requirement, options, criteria, and supporting evidence
  • Selected option and rationale
  • Security, privacy, operational, and cost consequences
  • Risks, mitigations, residual risk owner, and review date
  • Approvers, approval evidence, and implementation links
  • Required for services, SKUs, regions, identity, networking, telemetry, Agent scope, data, cost, deployment, and exceptions
Implementation records
  • Work item, repository, branch, commit, and pull request
  • Infrastructure module, parameters, target scope, and region
  • Predeployment checks, approvals, command or pipeline, and timestamps
  • Resources and configuration changed without exposing secrets
  • Validation commands, results, problems, corrections, and rollback
  • Implementer and independent reviewer
  • Manual changes prohibited unless approved and reconciled into infrastructure as code
Security and privacy
  • Managed identities instead of embedded credentials
  • Least privilege at the narrowest practical scope
  • Principal, role, scope, justification, owner, and expiration register
  • No secrets, tokens, connection strings, or customer data in source or public pages
  • Storage public access, TLS, networking, telemetry classification, and public endpoint approval
  • Public API metadata review and final access review
Cost and consumption
  • Trial versus post-trial charges and fixed versus active usage
  • Model multiplier, telemetry, compute, storage, network, tax, currency, and region assumptions
  • Approved budget, alert recipients, tested notifications, and Agent allocation
  • Weekly Cost Management ownership
  • Trial end, decision date, automatic teardown, and manual fallback
  • Funded approval before production continuation
Operational readiness
  • Ownership, support boundaries, health, telemetry, alert, and dashboard access
  • Severity, escalation, investigation, and recovery runbooks
  • Continuity, regional, change, rollback, emergency, capacity, and quota procedures
  • Cost monitoring, teardown, limitations, deferred work, access transfer, and knowledge transfer
  • Execution evidence for all ten reliability scenarios
Handover and customer acceptance
  • Source, architecture, inventory, decisions, implementation records, and runbooks transferred
  • Customer owners trained and access confirmed
  • Open risks and actions accepted with owners and dates
  • Customer approval or rejection recorded
  • Funded continuation or verified teardown completed

Definition of done

All 11 gates are mandatory
Customer confirms the agreed business outcomes were evaluated.
Deployed inventory matches the approved architecture.
Every material decision has an approved decision record.
Every implementation has deployment and validation evidence.
Security, privacy, RBAC, and public-access reviews are complete.
Cost, consumption, and post-trial implications are acknowledged.
Alerts, dashboards, scenarios, and recovery procedures are validated.
Open risks have named owners and target dates.
Runbooks, source, access, and knowledge are handed over.
The customer signs the acceptance record.
Funded continuation or teardown is verified.

Exception management

No silent or permanent exceptions
An undocumented exception is a failed requirement.

Exceptions are temporary, risk-owned, approved, time-bound, and linked to remediation.

Partner commitment

We will maintain transparent and traceable records for every customer engagement, material decision, Azure implementation, validation result, security assignment, cost control, operational procedure, exception, handover activity, and acceptance decision. We will not represent an engagement as complete until the required evidence is available and the customer has confirmed the outcome.
